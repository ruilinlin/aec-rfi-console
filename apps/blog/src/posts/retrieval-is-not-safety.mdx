On a construction site, a Request for Information (RFI) is how a contractor asks the design
team to resolve an ambiguity: *how far apart can these fasteners be, which revision governs,
can we substitute this product?* The answer gets built into the building. A wrong answer is
rework, a claim, or a safety defect — which makes RFIs a brutal benchmark for retrieval-augmented
generation: the answer must be **cited**, the citation must come from the **governing revision**,
and when the spec is silent the only correct answer is **"the spec is silent."**

I built [aec-rfi-agent](https://github.com/ruilinlin/aec-rfi-agent) to test one question: how much
of that safety comes from retrieval quality, and how much from what happens after retrieval?

## The system in one paragraph

A LangGraph pipeline over 12 UFGS specification sections (1,524 chunks in PostgreSQL + pgvector,
voyage-3.5 embeddings, claude-sonnet-5 drafting): **classify** the RFI and extract section-code
filters → **retrieve** with hybrid search (dense cosine + keyword tsvector, fused by Reciprocal
Rank Fusion) → **cross_check** every candidate against a supersedes graph parsed from the
documents themselves → **draft** an answer that may only cite provided chunks → **verify** each
quote verbatim against the source and review every claim. If verification fails, the output is
not a worse answer — it is a `GapReport` naming what was searched and why nothing governs.

## The experiment

RFI-Bench: 40 test items (clarification, conflict, substitution, and unanswerable traps with
near-miss sections), drafted with LLM assistance and verified item by item against the corpus.
The test split was frozen before any test run, used exactly once, and never for tuning. Seven
configurations ran in a single pass; failed items (2 of 280, truncation-class) were recorded and
excluded, and clean configs were never re-run.

The row to stare at is **b2**. Hybrid retrieval alone achieves *perfect* recall — every gold
chunk reaches the model — and respectable citation quality. And yet it cites a superseded
revision on **62%** of items where an older revision exists, and it answers **every single
unanswerable trap**. Retrieval found the right text; nothing told the system which text was
allowed to govern, or that declining was an option.

<Finding label="Thesis" title="Perfect retrieval, unsafe answers">
  Recall@10 of 100% coexists with a 62% stale-citation rate and a 0% abstention rate. Retrieval
  quality and answer safety are different axes; the second one is built in the layers after
  retrieval.
</Finding>

## What each layer buys, isolated

The ablations cut one layer at a time from the full system:

- **Remove cross_check** (the deterministic supersedes-graph gate): stale rate jumps 0% → 86%.
  One layer of governance is worth 86 points of revision safety — and it costs 8 milliseconds,
  because it is a graph lookup, not an LLM call.
- **Remove verify** (verbatim quote check + claim review): abstention collapses 88% → 0%. The
  verify node *is* the abstention mechanism; without it the agent answers everything, confidently.
- **Remove section-aware chunking** (fall back to fixed 1,500-char windows): recall drops 41
  points and citation precision 47. Chunks that respect clause boundaries are what make a
  citation like "09 51 00 / 3.1.5" possible at all.

The baseline with nothing after retrieval, b0, sets the floor: asked for a paint color with no
color schedule in the corpus, it invented "Sherwin-Williams SW 7036 'Accessible Beige'" — a
specific, plausible, fabricated answer. That is the failure mode every layer above exists to
prevent.

A `GapReport` is not an error state. It names the sections searched, the closest candidates, and
why none of them governs — which is exactly the information a design team needs to route the RFI
to a human. The cost of this conservatism on the dev set was one false abstention on a borderline
item with a typo in its source table; in construction, a deferral costs a day while a wrong
answer costs a rework order, so the conservative side of the frontier is the right side to err on.

## Honest numbers

Every response carries its own telemetry: a real run costs about **$0.02 and 5–20 seconds**,
with verification dominating latency and the deterministic cross_check adding ~8ms. Governance
is nearly free; verification is the cost — and the thing it buys, per the table above, is the
entire difference between a search engine and something you can hand a draft answer to.
